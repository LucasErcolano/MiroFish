Peronism in all its forms unexpectedly lost in 18 of Argentina’s 24 provinces, taking 31.7% of the vote, a figure that could rise slightly when smaller allied groups are counted. The result will allow the movement to retain its 99 seats in the lower house come December, but it risks losing its status as the largest minority if La Libertad Avanza adds its 80 deputies to the 24 from Pro, the party of former president Mauricio Macri (2015–2019). In the Senate, Peronism fared worse: it will hold 28 seats, down from 34, while La Libertad Avanza will grow from six to 18 senators. The big losers were the six governors who unsuccessfully tried to create an alternative to both the far-right and Peronism under the banner of a new coalition called United Provinces.
