# **Formative Seed-Data Analysis for Argentina’s 2025 Political-Economic Trajectory: A Qualitative Forecasting Pilot**

## **1\. Introduction to the Analytical Framework and Simulation Parameters**

The application of multi-agent systems and Large Language Models (LLMs) to qualitative political forecasting represents a frontier in predictive political economy. However, the integrity of any such simulation rests entirely upon the rigorous chronological quarantine of its initialization data. To accurately model and evaluate the predictive capabilities of distinct simulated personas—acting as political factions, economic sectors, and institutional bodies—the system must be anchored to a strictly defined temporal state. This comprehensive report establishes the foundational parameters for the "MiroFish" forecasting pilot, a simulation designed to predict Argentina's political and economic trajectory culminating in the October 2025 midterm legislative elections.  
The temporal boundary for this simulation is irrevocably fixed at 31 January 2025\. All systemic conditions, actor incentives, macroeconomic indicators, public opinion metrics, and institutional architectures must reflect exclusively the intelligence and data available on or before this precise date. Any subsequent developments—most notably the actual seat distributions resulting from the October 2025 legislative elections, the mid-to-late 2025 veto overrides, and late-2025 macroeconomic shifts—constitute the "ground truth." This ground truth will be kept strictly quarantined from the initialization prompt and will serve solely as the evaluative benchmark against which the multi-agent system's forecasts will be measured. Integrating post-cutoff data into the seed environment would induce catastrophic chronological contamination, thereby nullifying the scientific validity of the forecasting pilot.  
As of the initialization date in January 2025, the Argentine Republic presents a highly complex, asymmetrical, and unprecedented political environment. President Javier Milei and his nascent libertarian coalition, La Libertad Avanza (LLA), have concluded their first year in power. This inaugural year was defined by the aggressive application of macroeconomic stabilization measures and severe fiscal austerity, executed under the governing philosophy that the state possessed no residual capital for public expenditure.1 Despite holding a profound structural minority in both chambers of the National Congress—falling well short of even an impeachment-proof threshold—the administration has successfully leveraged executive decrees, a fragmented opposition, and an ad-hoc coalition of center-right allies to sustain its radical agenda.1  
The defining analytical question for the multi-agent forecasting system is whether the administration's precarious legislative shield—specifically the critical one-third minority required to sustain presidential vetoes against opposition-sponsored spending bills—can hold against accumulating social pressures, territorial resource deprivation, and opposition realignment leading up to the October 2025 elections. This report provides an exhaustive curation of valid pre-cutoff sources, a deep evaluation of the macroeconomic and institutional vectors, an architectural mapping of the relevant political actors, and a highly structured data evaluation table to serve as the definitive initialization environment for the forecasting agents.

## **2\. The Macroeconomic Initialization State: Shock Therapy and Structural Vulnerabilities**

To properly initialize the simulated agents representing the government, international markets, and the domestic electorate, the system must ingest the nuanced economic equilibrium that defined Argentina at the close of 2024 and the dawn of 2025\. The intelligence indicates a profound divergence between macroeconomic stabilization at the state level and microeconomic distress at the citizen level.

### **2.1 Fiscal Stabilization and the Eradication of the Deficit**

Upon assuming the presidency, Javier Milei inherited a nation teetering on the brink of hyperinflation, burdened by negative net foreign exchange reserves, heavily distorted and artificially frozen utility prices, and an out-of-control fiscal deficit.1 His administration's response was an immediate and drastic contraction of state expenditure. Over his first year, the government instituted aggressive budget cuts, paralyzed public works, and froze discretionary spending to combat the fiscal deficit.1 This fiscal shock therapy yielded significant macroeconomic milestones that must be encoded into the baseline of the forecasting agents.  
By the end of 2024, the administration had achieved the elimination of the chronic fiscal deficit, executing structural public spending cuts that amounted to a reduction of between 5 and 9 percentage points of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP).2 Consequently, inflation, which had registered an annual rate of 166% in November 2024, was forced into a strict downward trajectory. Private sector analysts and the International Monetary Fund (IMF) estimated that annual inflation would fall to approximately 62.7% for 2025, while the government's highly optimistic 2025 budget proposal projected a drop to 18%.1 The IMF noted in its December 2024 staff report that the strong disinflation process was continuing, supported by a strong fiscal anchor and a strengthened monetary framework.5  
Financial markets reacted with exuberance to the return of fiscal discipline, providing the executive with a powerful narrative of success. Argentine stocks delivered the highest yearly returns of any economy globally in 2024, doubling the returns of the second-best performing international markets.1 Sovereign risk, measured through emerging market bond index spreads, plummeted to around 750 basis points by early December 2024, and the gap between the official and the parallel exchange rates narrowed to single digits.1 Furthermore, a highly successful tax amnesty scheme implemented by the administration managed to attract substantial undeclared foreign currency deposits back into the formal banking system, temporarily bolstering domestic liquidity.1 Following a severe contraction, the Argentine economy officially emerged from recession in the third quarter of 2024, with the IMF projecting a robust 5% economic growth rate for 2025 following an estimated contraction of roughly 3.5% in 2024\.1

### **2.2 Debt Obligations, Foreign Reserves, and the Currency Controls (The "Cepo")**

Despite the celebratory reaction of the financial markets, the forecasting models must account for the severe structural constraints and liquidity traps that remained fully active as of January 2025\. Argentina's net international reserves remained deeply negative, hovering at approximately negative $3 billion at the end of 2024\.1 This lack of hard currency reserves is the critical vulnerability for the administration, as the nation faces massive external debt servicing obligations throughout the forecasting period. Argentina is scheduled to make a $3 billion interest payment to the IMF, alongside massive private bond payments approaching $4.3 billion due in both January and July 2025\.4  
Because of these depleted reserves, the "cepo"—the highly restrictive web of capital and currency controls implemented by previous administrations—remained firmly in place as of the January 2025 cutoff. The cepo acts as a primary deterrent to the foreign direct investment necessary to transition from mere fiscal stabilization to genuine economic growth and job creation.1 For the forecasting agents representing the executive branch, ending the currency controls without triggering an inflationary devaluation of the peso is the ultimate strategic dilemma. The administration is highly aware that lifting the cepo could drive investment, but doing so prematurely risks depreciating the peso and reaccelerating inflation—a catastrophic political risk that the government will likely attempt to defer until after the October 2025 midterm elections.1 Furthermore, there is a risk associated with the overvaluation of the Argentine peso; despite an inflation rate of 117% over the previous year, the peso depreciated by less than 30%, leaving it at one of its strongest real levels in decades, which threatens export competitiveness.3

### **2.3 The RIGI and International Alignments**

To bypass the limitations of the cepo and attract desperately needed foreign capital, the administration enacted the Incentive Regime for Large Investments (RIGI) in July 2024\.1 The mechanics of the RIGI must be deeply integrated into the AI simulation as the government's primary growth catalyst. The RIGI is designed to isolate foreign and domestic investors from the pervasive structural risks of the Argentine economy by providing a highly secure, legally binding environment for major capital projects exceeding $200 million.1 Investors utilize sole-purpose vehicles to gain 30-year stability on tax, foreign exchange, and customs duties.1 Crucially, the scheme lowers the federal income tax from 35% to 25%, eliminates export taxes after three years, and entirely exempts these mega-projects from local capital controls and trade restrictions.1 Initial successes under this regime include a $2.5 billion commitment from Rio Tinto for lithium mining and a $3 billion investment for a new oil pipeline.1  
On the geopolitical front, the administration has pivoted sharply. President Milei has positioned himself as an ardent supporter of the incoming US President Donald Trump, explicitly seeking a free trade agreement with the United States and hoping this ideological alignment will prompt the IMF board to restructure Argentina’s outstanding $40 billion program under more favorable terms, potentially involving a $20 billion swap line.1 Concurrently, Argentina officially began its accession process to the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) to anchor its market reforms, lower its national risk rating, and restore institutional credibility.1 Despite Milei's previous ideological hostility toward China, the geopolitical reality of negative reserves forced a pragmatic stabilization; Argentina renewed a $5 billion portion of its currency swap with China in June 2024, and Milei met with Xi Jinping, acknowledging China as a "very interesting trade partner".1

## **3\. Socioeconomic Friction: Poverty, Indigence, and the Tolerance for Austerity**

The macroeconomic triumphs celebrated by international markets were purchased at an exorbitant social cost. The multi-agent forecasting system must calculate the precise elasticity of the Argentine electorate to determine how long voters will tolerate profound microeconomic distress before punishing the incumbent government at the ballot box.

### **3.1 The Objective Deterioration of Living Standards**

The statistical manifestation of the administration's fiscal shock therapy is most acutely captured in the data released by the National Institute of Statistics and Census (INDEC) and the Social Debt Observatory of the Catholic University of Argentina (UCA). INDEC data revealed that poverty reached a staggering 52.9% of the population during the first semester of 2024, representing an increase of more than 14.4% compared to the previous semester.7 Indigence, which measures the segment of the population whose income is insufficient to cover even a basic food basket, surged to 18.1%.9  
By the third quarter of 2024, UCA's Social Debt Observatory reported a slight stabilization, with poverty retreating marginally to 49.9% and indigence settling at 12.3%.10 However, while this represented a subtle improvement from the absolute peak of the crisis earlier in the year (which hit nearly 55%), the levels remained substantially higher than the 44.7% poverty rate registered at the end of 2023\.11 The demographic breakdown of this poverty is the most volatile variable for future social stability: UCA reported that 65.5% of children and adolescents between the ages of 0 and 17 were living below the poverty line, with 2 out of every 10 children living in extreme poverty.11  
The day-to-day reality of this adjustment translated into severe consumer behavioral shifts. The UCA survey revealed that 29.4% of households were forced to cut spending on essential medications, 29.9% ceased paying local taxes, and 27.1% defaulted on utility services.12 This microeconomic reality creates a highly combustible environment. A qualitative forecasting agent representing civil society or trade unions would logically interpret these indicators as a mandate for aggressive mobilization and strikes against the government's economic program.

### **3.2 The Paradox of Public Opinion and Approval Ratings**

Despite the objective deterioration in poverty metrics and real wages, public opinion data from December 2024 and January 2025 revealed a paradoxical stability that defies traditional political science models of "economic voting." President Milei closed his first year with an approval rating hovering around 54%, as measured by the Universidad de San Andrés (UdeSA), explicitly outperforming his predecessors Mauricio Macri and Alberto Fernández at the exact same chronological point in their mandates.2  
This resilience is further corroborated by December 2024 polling from CB Consultora. When asked about the economic trajectory of their households since Javier Milei took office, 54.5% of respondents explicitly stated that their economy had worsened, compared to 44.1% who felt it had improved.13 Yet, this acknowledgment of personal economic pain did not translate into a commensurate drop in presidential approval. The electorate remained heavily polarized between "anti-Kirchnerista" and "anti-Mileista" identities.14 The government’s approval floor is driven by a deep-seated, systemic public rejection of the previous Peronist political class, which voters still hold responsible for the underlying structural crisis.1  
This creates a unique predictive vector for the LLM agents: the electorate is currently exhibiting a profound "tolerance for austerity," accepting immediate socioeconomic pain under the belief that it is the necessary cure for decades of mismanagement.1 The central forecasting question is whether this narrative of "paying the cost for previous sins" holds an expiration date that falls before or after the October 2025 ballot. If the promised economic rebound does not materialize in labor markets, voter fatigue may set in, causing the middle class—currently absorbing massive utility tariff hikes—to drift back toward the opposition.1

## **4\. The Institutional Crucible: Congress, Vetoes, and the 1/3 Threshold**

The most critical institutional dynamic that the forecasting pilot must model is the severe asymmetry of power within the National Congress. To prevent the simulation from generating hallucinated capabilities for the executive branch, the precise parliamentary mathematics of late 2024 must be strictly enforced as the baseline.

### **4.1 The Minority Government and Defensive Strategy**

La Libertad Avanza (LLA) entered office with a minuscule fraction of seats in both the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate, rendering the executive branch structurally weak and incapable of passing legislation unilaterally.1 The administration lacks not only a governing majority but also an impeachment-proof minority. Consequently, the 2024 legislative year was characterized by historically low output; Congress sanctioned only four laws throughout the entire year, the lowest output in four years, while only three out of twenty-six bills sent by the Executive were approved (including the vital omnibus *Ley Bases*).2  
To bypass this legislative paralysis, President Milei deployed an aggressively defensive strategy, relying on the continuation of the emergency executive decree (DNU 70/2023) and the constitutional power of the presidential veto.2 Because the administration cannot proactively legislate its agenda, its primary goal in Congress is to block the opposition from passing laws that mandate new public spending, which would dismantle the zero-deficit fiscal anchor.

### **4.2 The Mechanics of the Veto Shield: The "87 Heroes"**

The architectural linchpin of Milei’s governance survival is the "one-third threshold." Under the Argentine Constitution, if the President vetoes a piece of legislation, the Congress can override that veto and force the law into effect only if they secure a two-thirds supermajority in both chambers. Therefore, the executive does not need to win majority votes; it merely needs to control one-third plus one of the legislators to create an impenetrable "veto shield."  
This exact mathematical dynamic played out twice in late 2024, providing the perfect behavioral template for the forecasting agents. In early September 2024, the opposition successfully passed a law increasing retirement pensions, a move that the government argued would destroy the fiscal surplus.15 President Milei immediately vetoed the legislation. To sustain this veto in the Chamber of Deputies, the government required 86 votes to block the opposition's two-thirds override attempt. On September 11, despite massive protests outside Congress that resulted in police deploying pepper spray and rubber bullets, the government secured exactly 87 votes to sustain the veto, while the opposition mustered 153 votes.15 President Milei celebrated these 87 legislators on social media, controversially labeling them as "heroes" and "patriots" who put a stop to "fiscal degenerates".15  
A month later, in October 2024, the administration deployed this exact same mechanism to veto the University Funding Law. Once again, the Chamber of Deputies convened to challenge the veto. The opposition secured 159 votes to override, but the government managed to hold 85 votes in favor of the veto (with 5 abstentions), effectively preventing the opposition from reaching the two-thirds requirement, thereby killing the university funding increase for at least a year.17  
For the forecasting agents, these 87 to 85 deputies represent the definitive metric of executive survival. This blocking minority is not composed entirely of LLA members; it relies heavily on the center-right PRO party (allied with former President Mauricio Macri) and splintered factions of the Unión Cívica Radical (UCR) who break ranks with their own party leadership to support the government.15 If the simulation agents calculate that electoral pressures in 2025 will cause the PRO or UCR to abandon this coalition, the executive will face a cascade of unfunded legislative mandates, threatening the core fiscal anchor of the entire economic program.

## **5\. Territorial Tensions and Opposition Realignment**

The simulation must also accurately reflect the territorial and partisan struggles occurring outside of the presidential palace, as these dynamics directly influence the composition and loyalty of the National Congress.

### **5.1 The Defunding of the Provinces**

The relationship between the national executive and the 24 provincial governors was highly strained as of January 2025\. A significant portion of Milei's fiscal adjustment was achieved by slashing direct real investment (completely halting federal public works) and eliminating discretionary capital transfers to the provinces.2 This forced provincial governors to implement severe, localized austerity measures and assume the political cost of failing infrastructure and diminished local services.  
The administration promoted a policy of decentralization, attempting to transfer unfinished public works directly to local jurisdictions, but provided no corresponding funding.2 However, despite this aggressive financial squeeze, governors found it exceedingly difficult to mount a unified opposition to the President. Because Milei retained a 54% approval rating nationally, local governors were politically constrained from confronting him directly, fearing backlash from their own electorates. The AI agents representing the governors must therefore balance their desperation for fiscal resources against the political risk of attacking a popular president.2

### **5.2 The Reorganization of Peronism**

The opposition landscape as of January 2025 was dominated by the internal restructuring of the Peronist movement (Partido Justicialista \- PJ). Still reeling from their catastrophic electoral defeat in 2023, the movement initiated a bitter leadership battle. Former President Cristina Fernández de Kirchner maneuvered to take formal, absolute control of the PJ, publishing letters indicating she was ready to accept the challenge of leading the party.19  
However, her leadership was not universally accepted. She faced internal challenges from orthodox provincial leaders, most notably La Rioja Governor Ricardo Quintela, who publicly announced his intention to compete for the party presidency, arguing for a process of unity that did not exclude alternative voices.19 This tension between the Kirchnerist faction centered in the Province of Buenos Aires (led by Governor Axel Kicillof) and the federal, provincial Peronist leaders threatens to dilute their electoral effectiveness. A forecasting agent representing the opposition must calculate whether capitalizing on the 49.9% poverty rate requires a unified front, or whether internal fragmentation will inadvertently aid the ruling LLA party by splitting the opposition vote in the 2025 midterms.

## **10\. Analytical Synthesis and Predictive Forecasting Vectors**

The initialization state of Argentina as of 31 January 2025 provides a deeply contradictory, highly volatile environment, making it an ideal crucible for testing the limits of qualitative political forecasting via LLM agents. The system is provided with an executive administration executing the most radical pro-market state contraction in modern Argentine history, possessing immense popular legitimacy, yet lacking the basic institutional machinery traditionally required to ensure governability.  
To successfully forecast the outcome of the October 2025 elections, the MiroFish multi-agent system must calculate the complex intersection of three primary analytical vectors.  
First, the **Macroeconomic Vector**: Will the aggressive suppression of inflation, the elimination of the fiscal deficit, and the attempted accumulation of foreign reserves through the RIGI be sufficient to allow the administration to lift the "cepo" capital controls before the electoral cycle peaks? If the cepo is lifted too early, the resulting currency devaluation will spike inflation, annihilating the administration's primary political achievement. If it is lifted too late, the lack of foreign investment will deepen the recession, accelerating job losses.  
Second, the **Social Tolerance Vector**: Can an electorate, of which 49.9% lives in poverty and 65.5% of its children live below the poverty line, maintain its rejection of the prior political establishment long enough for structural macro-reforms to generate localized, microeconomic relief? The agents must calculate the exact breaking point where the pain of utility tariff hikes and wage stagnation overrides the ideological desire to punish the previous Peronist administration.  
Finally, the **Institutional Vector**: Will the fragile, defensive alliance of exactly 87 deputies hold firm, protecting the executive from opposition-led fiscal sabotage until the October midterms? The PRO and UCR factions will constantly calculate whether shielding Milei's vetoes benefits their own electoral prospects or drags them down into irrelevance.  
By strictly adhering to the curated pre-cutoff seed data established in this report, and by mercilessly isolating the system from post-cutoff chronological contamination, the MiroFish pilot is perfectly positioned to test whether synthetic intelligence can navigate these contradictory vectors and logically deduce the trajectory of South America's second-largest economy.
