# **Adversarial Audit Report: Temporal Contamination and Source Reliability for MiroFish Forecasting Pilot (Argentina 2025\)**

## **The Epistemological Framework of Temporal Auditing in Qualitative Forecasting**

In the architecture of qualitative forecasting models, particularly those leveraging large language models within Retrieval-Augmented Generation architectures, the integrity of the predictive simulation relies entirely on the absolute impermeability of the temporal boundary. For the MiroFish forecasting pilot concerning the macroeconomic and political trajectory of Argentina throughout 2025, the temporal boundary is defined with strict rigidity: the cutoff date is exactly 31 January 2025\. Any ingress of data, metadata, or contextual synthesis generated after this threshold constitutes temporal contamination, fundamentally degrading the predictive validity of the simulation into mere hindsight bias.  
The mandate of this adversarial audit is to execute a merciless exclusion of any data vector capable of breaching the temporal quarantine. Forecasting models are highly susceptible to micro-leakages. A single post-cutoff document, an updated metadata tag, or a dynamically updating web widget embedded in an otherwise pre-cutoff page can transmit future market states, policy outcomes, or statistical revisions back into the simulation environment. When an autoregressive language model processes even a fragment of future context, its probability distribution shifts, aligning its generated "forecasts" with the known future rather than inferring outcomes from the constrained prior state.  
This audit evaluates the candidate seed corpus against a zero-tolerance framework for temporal leakage. The evaluation protocols necessitate a rigid distinction between the moment of data collection, the timestamp of publication, and the permanence of the hosting infrastructure. Documents that exist as static, immutable objects prior to the cutoff are preserved. Documents hosted on dynamic content management systems, living wikis, or news portals with silent update mechanisms are treated as highly suspicious or entirely excluded. Furthermore, the audit recognizes the inherent lag in macroeconomic reporting, a critical factor in the Argentine context. Consequently, post-cutoff sources containing data referencing pre-cutoff periods are systematically isolated for use exclusively as ground-truth verification files, ensuring they never intersect with the predictive input streams.  
The complexity of auditing the Argentine macroeconomic environment is compounded by the rapid pace of policy implementation under the administration of Javier Milei. Following his inauguration in December 2023, the administration executed profound fiscal adjustments, targeted deregulation, and altered the exchange rate regime.1 A forecaster operating strictly on January 31, 2025, possesses a very specific, limited view of how these policies are unfolding. They have access to the immediate recessionary impacts and the initial disinflationary trends of 2024, but they remain entirely blind to the midterm elections of late 2025, the subsequent agricultural harvest yields, and the long-term sustainability of the fiscal anchor. Preserving this blindness is the supreme objective of this audit.

## **Verification and Stratification of Pre-Cutoff Seed Architecture**

The documents classified within the approved tier exhibit static finality prior to the January 31, 2025 cutoff. These consist primarily of institutional reports published as immutable files, preserving the exact epistemological state of the authors at the time of release. These sources form the foundational context window for the forecasting model, providing the necessary historical and current-state variables required to project future states.  
The International Monetary Fund's early 2024 reviews form a robust historical baseline. The Seventh Review of the Extended Arrangement Under the Extended Fund Facility, completed in January 2024 and published in February 2024, establishes the initial programmatic parameters of the new administration.2 This document details the immediate disbursement of $4.7 billion to support the authorities' efforts to correct severe policy slippages from the final quarters of 2023\.2 It outlines the ambitious stabilization plan centered on establishing a strong fiscal anchor, bringing down inflation, rebuilding reserves, and tackling distortions.2 This document is perfectly temporally insulated and provides the foundational architecture of the government's strategy.  
Building upon this, the IMF's Eighth Review, published in June 2024, provides a mid-year check-in on the progress of the stabilization plan.4 This report enables a disbursement of approximately $800 million and confirms that the program remained firmly on track, with all quantitative performance criteria for end-March 2024 met with margins.4 The document highlights the necessity of improving the quality of fiscal adjustment and taking initial steps toward an enhanced monetary and foreign exchange policy framework.4 Because this document was finalized and published well before the cutoff, its ingestion presents zero risk of temporal contamination while supplying the simulation with critical mid-2024 macroeconomic variables and policy directives.  
At the supranational level, the World Bank's Global Economic Prospects report, released in January 2025, constitutes an ideal seed document.9 It captures the exact forward-looking institutional consensus immediately prior to the cutoff date. The report projects a 2.5 percent regional growth for Latin America and the Caribbean in 2025, explicitly noting that this growth is primarily driven by Argentina's anticipated recovery following two years of economic contraction.9 The document forecasts Argentina's growth to accelerate to approximately 4.8 percent annually as the country recovers, interest rates normalize, and inflation decreases.14 Crucially, because this report was finalized and released in January 2025, it serves as a perfectly timed snapshot of mainstream economic expectations. It does not represent what actually happened, but rather what the World Bank believed would happen as of the temporal cutoff.  
Similarly, the United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America and the Caribbean (ECLAC) Preliminary Overview of the Economies of Latin America and the Caribbean 2024 is equally secure for ingestion.17 Presented on December 18, 2024, this report projects a 2.4 percent regional expansion for 2025 and provides critical, untainted baseline estimates for the Argentine economic condition leading into the cutoff month.23 It notes that South America is expected to grow by 2.9 percent in 2025, driven by recoveries in Argentina, Bolivia, and Ecuador after their economies contracted in 2024\.23  
At the domestic level, the Argentine Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos (INDEC) Consumer Price Index (IPC) report for December 2024 is cleared for inclusion.25 Released in mid-January 2025, it confirms the December inflation rate at 2.7 percent, bringing the accumulated variation for the entire year of 2024 to 117.8 percent.25 This document provides the final piece of official backward-looking inflation data available to an analyst existing precisely on January 31, 2025\. It is the definitive baseline from which the model must forecast 2025 inflation trajectories.  
Further bolstering the domestic macro-fiscal baseline is the Banco Central de la República Argentina (BCRA) Informe Monetario Mensual for December 2024, published in January 2025\.27 This document provides the official central bank summary of monetary base dynamics and remunerated liabilities at the close of 2024\.27 It is temporally pristine and essential for establishing the monetary conditions precedent to the forecasting period.  
Finally, verified news articles that predate the cutoff provide crucial narrative context. A Reuters report dated January 29, 2025, detailing an interview with Federico Sturzenegger, Argentina's Deregulation and State Transformation Minister, offers late-stage pre-cutoff insights.28 The article confirms the administration's intent to execute deeper cuts in 2025, targeting industries from autos to medicines, and reiterates that fiscal balance is non-negotiable.28 Because this article was published exactly two days before the cutoff, it is highly relevant, provided that an archival link is utilized to prevent dynamic post-cutoff updates to the host page from bleeding into the simulation. Similarly, an AIM Digital report summarizing the BCRA's December Relevamiento de Expectativas de Mercado (REM) provides market consensus expectations for the January 2025 exchange rate (projected at 1,042 pesos per dollar).29

## **Dynamic Vulnerabilities and Suspicious Vectors Requiring Archival Isolation**

The secondary classification identifies sources that, while nominally associated with pre-cutoff topics, are hosted on infrastructures that present severe risks of dynamic alteration. The core vulnerability here is the "living document" paradigm, characterized by web pages designed to seamlessly update without modifying their primary uniform resource locator or distinctly altering their surface timestamp. Ingestion of these raw URLs by automated scraping tools guarantees the integration of post-cutoff data into the predictive simulation.  
Wikipedia pages covering the Milei administration represent catastrophic leakage risks.1 The encyclopedia's architecture is predicated on continuous integration and revision. The page detailing the "Economic reforms of Javier Milei" explicitly references outcomes that extend far beyond the January 2025 cutoff, noting that the administration achieved a budget surplus for the first time since 2011 and discussing long-term shifts in poverty rates and public confidence.1 If a web-scraper pulls this live URL, it ingests a synthetic history of the entire administration, completely neutralizing the model's forecasting function. These pages can only be approved if a specific, hardcoded OldID permalink representing a revision stamped prior to 23:59 UTC on January 31, 2025, is isolated and utilized.  
Similarly, institutional landing pages present extreme hazards due to their reliance on dynamic content delivery networks and embedded application programming interfaces. The World Bank's Argentina overview page contains dynamic update fields indicating modifications as late as October 2025\.31 While the core text might reference early 2024 projects, the page also dynamically renders recent approvals, effectively leaking the reality of late-2025 institutional financing into the model. Data dashboards relying on client-side rendering frameworks, such as the World Bank's Highcharts implementation for Argentina's economic indicators, actively pull the latest available databases via an API upon page load.32 An analyst or an automated script accessing the page in mid-2025 or 2026 will see data extending to the end of 2025, effectively destroying the simulation's integrity.32  
The International Monetary Fund's country landing page for Argentina operates under similar dynamic principles.33 The page is designed to surface the most recent reports and press releases. Attempting to scrape this page to discover the Seventh or Eighth Reviews will inadvertently capture links and summaries for the post-cutoff First and Second Reviews of the new arrangement.34 Consequently, institutional landing pages must be categorically excluded in favor of direct links to the immutable PDF reports they host.  
The official portal for the BCRA's Relevamiento de Expectativas de Mercado presents an identical structural flaw.36 The index page dynamically updates to list the most recent monthly reports, displaying links to surveys from the latter half of 2025 and early 2026\.36 Scraping this index page directly injects the knowledge that these reports exist into the model, a subtle but significant temporal breach.  
YouTube sources and other multimedia platforms pose a distinct metadata risk.38 While a video detailing the Senate passage of a key reform bill in June 2024 contains pristine, pre-cutoff audiovisual content, the surrounding Document Object Model creates an unacceptable surface area for post-cutoff data.39 The page structure includes algorithmic video recommendations based on current trends, updated view and subscriber counts, and dynamically populated comment sections that frequently feature retrospective analysis by users months or years after the video's publication. Extracting data from these sources requires extreme precision: the audio transcript must be manually verified and decoupled entirely from the webpage infrastructure before it can be considered for inclusion.

## **Quarantined Post-Cutoff Artifacts and Ground Truth Repositories**

The final classification represents data exhibiting explicit temporal violations. These documents were published, authored, or finalized after January 31, 2025\. While their inclusion in the predictive input space is strictly forbidden to prevent contamination, they possess immense utility for the evaluation phase of the simulation. These sources form the exclusive "Ground Truth" repository against which the forecasting pilot will be benchmarked and scored.  
A critical vector of temporal analysis involves navigating the delayed release mechanisms of macroeconomic indicators, often resulting in backdated data traps. The Banco Central de la República Argentina (BCRA) Relevamiento de Expectativas de Mercado (REM) corresponding to January 2025 is a perfect illustration of this hazard.40 The survey data mapping market expectations for January was collected from participants between January 29 and January 31, 2025\. Conceptually, the thoughts and estimations of these analysts existed prior to the cutoff. However, the official report synthesizing these views was strictly embargoed and published on February 6, 2025\.41 Because the document physically manifested post-cutoff, its ingestion is an explicit violation. It must be strictly reserved for ground truth, revealing to the evaluators that the market's inflation expectation for January settled at 2.3 percent.41  
The INDEC January 2025 Consumer Price Index report presents a similar temporal boundary condition.42 The official determination that the inflation rate dropped to 2.2 percent for January, marking the lowest monthly index since the pandemic, was not published until February 13, 2025, as corroborated by external reporting from the Buenos Aires Herald.42 Any forecasting simulation operating on a January 31 cutoff cannot "know" the January inflation rate; it must predict it based on the December baseline.25 Injecting the February 13 publication into the January 31 seed data collapses the predictive mechanism entirely.  
Extensive financial reporting from BBVA Research spanning March, June, and December 2025, as well as their 2026 outlooks, are heavily contaminated with post-cutoff realities and must be quarantined.44 The December 2025 Argentina Economic Outlook provides an incredibly detailed retrospective on the year's performance, noting that the midterm election results signaled renewed political backing for the administration's economic policy.44 It documents a primary surplus reaching 1.7 percent of GDP, full-year inflation decelerating to 30 percent, and an exchange rate moving to 1,510 pesos per dollar.44 It also confirms a GDP growth realization of 4.5 percent for the year.44 These figures are the precise targets the forecasting pilot is designed to predict. Allowing the BBVA reports into the simulation would provide the model with the absolute answers to the test.  
The International Monetary Fund's subsequent reviews under the newly negotiated arrangements further populate the ground truth repository. The First Review of the new Extended Fund Facility, finalized in July and published in August 2025, enables a disbursement of about $2 billion and verifies the smooth transition to a more flexible exchange rate regime.35 Furthermore, the existence of the new IMF program itself—a $20 billion deal approved on April 11, 2025, representing Argentina's 23rd program with the Fund—is a massive macroeconomic event that occurs entirely post-cutoff.48 As detailed in analysis by the Peterson Institute for International Economics, this deal marked a crucial victory for the administration.48 A model forecasting from January 31 must predict the likelihood and structure of this deal; it cannot be fed the April confirmation. The Second Review, published in May 2026, extends this validation horizon even further into the future.34  
Ancillary reporting referencing advancing agricultural data, such as a Zawya report projecting the 2025/26 soybean harvest to reach 50.1 million tons due to higher-than-expected yields, provides excellent sector-specific ground truth for export forecasting but clearly references data accumulated after the cutoff.50 Similarly, Trading Economics datasets showing inflation rates decreasing to 32.40 percent in April 2026 demonstrate the utility of long-tail databases for verifying multi-year model accuracy, provided they remain securely isolated from the input pipeline.51 Finally, sources completely irrelevant to the Argentine scope, such as a Reuters analysis of Caribbean, Cuban, and Venezuelan geopolitics, must be excluded not only for temporal risks but for diluting the semantic focus of the vector database.52

## **Reproducibility Checklist and Cryptographic Controls**

To guarantee that the forecasting pilot can be perfectly recreated and verified by independent adversarial entities, the entire state of the simulation must be cryogenically frozen. Without rigorous cryptographic and procedural controls, subsequent runs of the model might inadvertently pull cached data, updated API responses, or altered model weights, destroying the scientific validity of the qualitative forecast.  
**Cryptographic Hashes for Seed Immutability** must be implemented across the entire ingestion pipeline. Every single file deposited into the seed data directory must be hashed using the SHA-256 algorithm. These hashes must be recorded in an immutable ledger file. Any subsequent audit can immediately detect if a source document was silently replaced with a post-cutoff "corrected" version by comparing the current file hash against the original ledger. If the hashes do not match, the simulation is void.  
**Mandatory Usage of Downloaded PDFs over Live URLs** is a non-negotiable requirement for data retrieval. The ingestion script must never be permitted to reach out to the live internet during the simulation execution. If a model is fed a live URL, the HTTP request will pull the HTML state of the day the script is executed, not the state of January 31, 2025\. All approved documents must be downloaded, converted to flat PDF or plain text formats, and stored locally within the access-controlled seed directory.  
**Archive Links for Web Ephemera** are necessary for establishing the temporal provenance of news articles or HTML-native content. The raw text must be extracted, but an archival link utilizing services such as the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine must be explicitly timestamped on or before January 31, 2025, and saved in the manifest. This process cryptographically proves that the data existed in that exact linguistic sequence prior to the cutoff, eliminating the risk of stealth edits by publishers.  
**Comprehensive Metadata Sidecars** in JSON format must accompany each document. This metadata must meticulously detail the purported author, the date of data collection, the date of publication, the date of retrieval by the researcher, and the explicit justification for its temporal validity. This creates a transparent audit trail for every token of information fed into the vector database.  
**Strict Prompt Freezing** is required to prevent the introduction of bias through instruction manipulation. The system prompt, user prompt templates, and any few-shot examples used to prime the language model must be locked in a version-controlled text file. Iterative prompt engineering conducted post-cutoff can inadvertently inject hindsight bias if the prompt engineer subtly shapes the instructions based on their knowledge of what actually happened in late 2025, guiding the model toward the "correct" answer rather than letting it forecast organically.  
**Model Version and Parameter Freezing** finalizes the reproducible architecture. The specific neural network endpoint or localized weight file must be hardcoded. Using a rolling API tag guarantees that future runs will utilize a model that has undergone continuous alignment or pre-training on post-2025 data, obliterating the temporal wall. Furthermore, generation parameters such as temperature, top-p sampling limits, and the random seed integer must be fixed to ensure deterministic, or at least highly constrained, reproducibility.

## **Critical Vulnerability Vectors: Top 5 Temporal Leakage Risks**

Despite the implementation of strict directory structures and cryptographic hashing protocols, the nature of modern digital infrastructure presents insidious avenues for temporal leakage. The following five vectors represent the highest probability of catastrophic contamination specific to the macroeconomic and political forecasting of Argentina.  
The most severe risk emanates from the utilization of **Living URLs and Dynamic Content Injection**. Institutional landing pages and news hubs do not represent fixed documents; they are dynamic containers. An analyst verifying an overview page might read core text discussing early 2024 economic projects, but the periphery of the page—the sidebars, headers, and footers—frequently feature automated RSS feeds pulling late 2025 updates, or Javascript data visualization tools rendering late-2025 macroeconomic variables. If an automated scraping tool ingests the entire Document Object Model of this URL, the post-cutoff sidebar data becomes part of the pre-cutoff seed context. The model will seamlessly integrate this peripheral future data into its core reasoning, instantly corrupting the predictive constraints.  
A pervasive methodological error in dataset curation is the **Conflation of Access Date with Publication Date**. Many researchers implicitly trust the metadata generated by their operating system or web browser. However, the date a file was downloaded has zero epistemological bearing on the state of the knowledge contained within it. A document might have a PDF creation metadata tag indicating it was compiled in March 2026, but if the text itself was a static draft uploaded to an academic archive in January 2025, it remains temporally valid. Conversely, a news article bearing a prominent headline date of "January 2025" might contain a microscopic "Updated: April 2025" tag in the source code. The audit must ruthlessly enforce the publication and latest-revision dates while entirely ignoring the localized access date.  
In evaluating the Argentine context, the delay between economic activity and institutional reporting creates the **Economic Reporting Lag Trap**. The central bank's market expectations survey for January 2025 surveys participants strictly before the cutoff. Conceptually, the ideas within the report existed in the past. However, the report is synthesized and published in early February. A strict temporal boundary must exclude this document from the seed data. Allowing it into the simulation permits the model to "see" a synthesized, finalized institutional consensus that was physically impossible for an analyst to possess on January 31 at midnight. This backdated data provides the model with an unearned stabilizing anchor, artificially enhancing its predictive accuracy by feeding it the results of the period it is supposed to be forecasting.  
When relying on primary sources such as legislative sessions or political speeches hosted on multimedia platforms, analysts frequently encounter **Algorithmic Metadata Contamination**. The core video or audio asset of a June 2024 Senate vote is temporally fixed and highly relevant. However, scraping these pages via standard extraction APIs captures the surrounding metadata infrastructure. This includes algorithmic video recommendations generated by the platform's current, post-cutoff state, and most dangerously, user comment sections that frequently feature retrospective analyses discussing the long-term outcomes of the events depicted in the video. The only secure method for processing multimedia is to cleanly extract the raw audio transcript, manually stripping all surrounding platform architecture before depositing the isolated text into the seed directory.  
Finally, reliance on **Continuous Integration Platforms and Wiki Ecosystems** is fundamentally incompatible with forecasting methodologies. Platforms built on continuous, crowdsourced revision are fatal to temporal boundaries. A wiki page describing the administration's economic reforms is constantly mutating to reflect the latest realities. If an automated data pipeline pulls the live page during a model run conducted in mid-2026, it will ingest the entirety of the administration's historical outcomes, including late 2025 budget surpluses and poverty rate shifts, while the model is ostensibly trying to predict those exact variables for 2025\. The strict enforcement of archival permalinks or precise historical revision hashes is the only defense against this systemic contamination vector. Without this absolute isolation, the simulation ceases to operate as a forecast and devolves into an exercise in automated historical summarization.
