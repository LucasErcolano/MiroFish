# Bolivia runoff evidence package - Line 5 slim

Cutoff: 2025-10-17.

Use only the information below. Do not use the October 19, 2025 runoff result.

## BOL_T3_SLIM_01 - First-round context and runoff setup

Bolivia's 2025 presidential race took place amid economic stress, fuel scarcity, dollar shortages, inflation pressure and weakening confidence in the governing MAS. Before the August 17 first round, polling showed a fragmented race with no candidate close to the threshold needed to win outright. The runoff rule required more than 50 percent, or 40 percent with a 10-point lead, so a second round was plausible.

The first round produced a surprise. Rodrigo Paz finished first with roughly 32 percent and Jorge "Tuto" Quiroga finished second with just over 26 percent. MAS-linked candidates performed very poorly, each below 10 percent, leaving the governing party outside the runoff. Samuel Doria Medina finished third and later endorsed Paz.

## BOL_T3_SLIM_02 - Candidate profiles

Rodrigo Paz campaigned as a moderate reform candidate with the slogan "Capitalism for All." His platform emphasized decentralization, productive credit, transparency, and a less abrupt economic adjustment path. Paz rejected making the IMF the central answer, while leaving room for other multilateral support. His running mate Edman Lara brought an anti-corruption and outsider profile that had drawn attention in the first round.

Jorge "Tuto" Quiroga entered the runoff as a former president and a better-known conservative figure. His campaign emphasized a sharper break with MAS rule, pro-market reform, fiscal adjustment, fuel-subsidy reduction, trade liberalization, and possible support from the IMF and other lenders. Quiroga also signaled a clearer foreign-policy reset toward closer ties with the United States.

## BOL_T3_SLIM_03 - Late runoff polling

A late runoff poll published on October 13 reported Quiroga ahead of Paz by 44.9 percent to 36.5 percent, an 8.4-point gap. The poll appeared less than a week before the October 19 runoff and was framed as the latest electoral poll before the vote. It did not report the final result.

## BOL_T3_SLIM_04 - Foreign-policy signal

On October 15, US officials met with Bolivian presidential candidates and emphasized the possibility of improved relations after years of tension. The statement did not endorse a candidate and did not provide polling numbers. It showed that post-MAS foreign policy, economic stabilization and international financing were part of the late-campaign environment.
