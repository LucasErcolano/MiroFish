# Pre-first-round polling context

Published at: 2025-08-15

Source type: polling context

The final polling environment before Bolivia's August 17, 2025 first round remained fragmented and unstable. AtlasIntel published a national survey dated August 15, with fieldwork conducted from August 11 to August 14. The survey described itself as nationally representative and listed a sample size of 1,916 respondents with a margin of error of plus or minus 2 percentage points. It focused on voter preferences for the first round of the presidential election.

Local Bolivian coverage in early August also showed movement among the visible frontrunners. La Razon reported on August 8 that Jorge "Tuto" Quiroga had moved past Samuel Doria Medina into first place in one survey, nine days before the general election. That report presented Quiroga, Doria Medina and other opposition candidates as the central names in the race, with MAS-linked candidates weakened by party division and public dissatisfaction.

A second La Razon report, published August 10, described a Captura Consulting poll for Red Uno. In that survey, Samuel Doria Medina led with 21.6%, while Jorge Quiroga followed closely with 20.0%. The same report said the poll was conducted from July 27 to August 3 and included 2,500 interviews in urban, rural and Indigenous autonomous areas across Bolivia's nine departments, plus the city of El Alto.

These pre-election polls did not show a candidate near an outright first-round victory. The numbers clustered around the low twenties for the leading contenders, leaving space for late movement, undecided voters and polling error. The press coverage treated Doria Medina and Quiroga as major competitors, with different surveys showing different ordering at the top. The composition of a potential runoff was still uncertain.

The survey environment also reflected the difficulty of measuring a race shaped by protest voting, a fragmented governing party and changing voter alignments. Bolivia's opposition vote was not concentrated behind one figure before the first round. Several candidates claimed to represent change after MAS rule, and voters dissatisfied with the government still had multiple ways to express that preference.

Rodrigo Paz appeared in the race but was not consistently described as the principal frontrunner in the final pre-first-round coverage. The major polling question before election day was whether the visible opposition figures would consolidate support, whether left/MAS-linked candidates would retain enough votes to remain competitive, and how large the undecided or late-deciding electorate would be.

The available polling context therefore described a race with high uncertainty, close leading candidates, no first-round majority scenario and a likely second-round contest. It also established a baseline in which pre-election expectations were anchored more around Quiroga and Doria Medina than around a clear Paz-led race.
