Justificacion:
The simulation data strongly suggests a victory for Jorge "Tuto" Quiroga. Multiple sources, including Bolivia.com and Reuters, consistently report Quiroga leading in the polls (“Bolivia.com reports that Jorge "Tuto" Quiroga is ahead in a late runoff poll.”, "Reuters reported on the runoff between centrist senator Rodrigo Paz and conservative former president Jorge "Tuto" Quiroga.").  The analysis indicates that the economic crisis is a significant factor, and Quiroga’s proposal to seek support from the IMF (“Jorge Quiroga proposes seeking support from the IMF to stabilize the Bolivian economy.”) resonates with voters concerned about economic stability. Paz’s criticism of the MAS is acknowledged (“Paz criticizes MAS, stating their aspiration for social justice and economic sovereignty represents a temporary setback.”), but appears insufficient to overcome Quiroga’s momentum, particularly given the widespread dissatisfaction with the current economic situation.

The collapse of the MAS’s dominance is evident, with the runoff representing a “significant political shift after the worst electoral defeat for MAS in two decades” (Reuters). While Paz benefited from the endorsement of Samuel Doria Medina (“The endorsement of Samuel Doria Medina to Paz could shift the dynamic in the election.”), this appears to have provided only a marginal boost. The US engagement, while criticized by some (“Paz criticizes the US for interfering in Bolivia's democratic processes.”), generally seems to be viewed favorably by key segments of the electorate (“Samuel Doria Medina supports the statement made by US officials regarding a constructive relationship with Bolivia.”), contributing to a perception of Quiroga as a more pragmatic and internationally-aligned candidate. The fact that US officials have met with both candidates (“US officials have met with Jorge Quiroga.”, “US officials have met with Rodrigo Paz.”) suggests a willingness to work with either outcome, but the generally positive tone surrounding the engagement appears to benefit Quiroga.

Incertidumbre:
- The possibility of a last-minute shift in voter sentiment, particularly among undecided voters, remains a factor.
- The accuracy of the polls themselves is always subject to some degree of uncertainty, even though multiple sources confirm Quiroga’s lead.
- Unexpected external events, such as a sudden economic shock or a major political scandal, could influence the outcome.
