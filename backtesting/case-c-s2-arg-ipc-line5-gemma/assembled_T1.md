## T0 carryover

Argentina enters 2025 with a fragile disinflation/recovery scenario, severe social stress, and a Congress where the Executive can sustain vetoes only if it keeps a blocking coalition. Poverty, public opinion, and institutional constraints should interact with macro policy rather than be modeled as separate background facts [SOCIAL_01; POLL_01; INST_01; INST_02; MACRO_01].

## New evidence at T1

The BCRA REM for December 2024, published on `2025-01-07`, gives a market-expectations anchor before the final cutoff. It points to expected 2025 growth around 4.5% and expected 2025 inflation around 25.9%, plus exchange-rate, fiscal, and monetary expectations. This introduces a clearer quantitative expectation baseline for the IPC trajectory [MACRO_03].

The IMF Ex-post Evaluation, published on `2025-01-10`, adds external constraints: reserves, exchange-rate normalization, dismantling controls, real interest rates, fiscal consistency, and the social cost of adjustment. The IMF should be modeled as a constraint/agent: support can improve confidence and reserves, but conditionality pushes toward policy consistency and eventual FX normalization [GEO_01].

## Updated causal picture

At T1, MiroFish should update from a broad macro/social baseline to a more structured macro program:

- expected 2025 inflation is materially lower than 2024, but not guaranteed [MACRO_03];
- the adjustment has external credibility and IMF scrutiny, but reserves and FX controls remain central risks [GEO_01];
- social and congressional constraints can still break the linear disinflation story [SOCIAL_01; INST_01].

## Risks to simulate

- REM expectations may anchor agents, but they are forecasts rather than outcomes [MACRO_03].
- IMF constraints make exchange-rate normalization and reserves more important [GEO_01].
- Social stress and Congress remain the political channels through which the macro program can weaken [SOCIAL_01; INST_01].
